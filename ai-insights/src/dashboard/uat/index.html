1. What this review is for

Describe the work you want to review and what you know about effort, available time and prerequisites. You can use a detailed schedule, a high-level outline, notes or discussions with the people doing the work.

Describe the work

Choose an outcome or portion of work and a time period. For example: prepare a research report, update guidance, organize an event or introduce a new process.

Provide what you know

Choose relevant checks and enter estimates or confirmations. If detail is missing, leave it unknown. No particular planning tool, file or document is required.

Use the results

Receive explained findings, a list of information gaps and suggested next steps. Record actions, revise your plan and rerun the checks. You can download the review.

What if I only have an outline or rough estimates?

Start with the work and dates you know. Ask the people doing the work for remaining hours and availability. Dates or percent complete alone do not tell us how much work remains. An unknown value produces a preparation gap, not an assumption or a failed project.

Choose “Not sure” when you do not know whether a check applies. Choose “Not applicable” only when you can explain why it is irrelevant to this work.

3. Choose checks and provide what you know

You do not need every check. For each area, choose whether to include it, leave it undecided or exclude it with a reason. Missing detail will be identified in the results; it will not be invented.

Time available for the work

Compare the work still needed with the time people can provide. This is useful only when both totals cover the same work and period.

Example: “This review covers handoff readiness only; workload is outside its scope.” Excluding this check does not establish sufficient time.

Estimate unfinished work within your review period. Examples: 24 hours to review sources, 40 to draft guidance, or 12 to prepare training materials. Add people’s work hours; do not use elapsed days or percent complete as a substitute.

Hours people with the needed skills can give this work in the same period. Example: one person can give 8 hours and another 6, totaling 14. Allow for leave and other commitments. Zero means confirmed no availability.

Explain what is included so the comparison can be reviewed. Example: “Two authors estimated 20 hours each; only 30 hours are available after other assignments.” Avoid counting the same work or person twice.

Briefly describe who confirmed the figures and how. Example: “Authors confirmed remaining tasks and availability in Tuesday’s discussion.” Notes, observations or records are acceptable; no file name is requested or retrieved.

The check compares totals. It does not prove specialist availability, task sequencing or delivery feasibility.

Things needed before work can start

Check timing: what must be available before another task can start? Examples: permission to interview participants, approved text before publication, or a booked room before an event.

“Not sure” leaves this undecided. “Not applicable” records an exclusion, not a passed check.

Example: “This work can proceed independently; no prerequisite input or approval is needed.”

Work or information passed between people

Check readiness: can someone receive and use what another person provides? Examples: research notes handed to an analyst, a draft passed to a reviewer, or materials delivered to an event team. This is separate from checking dates.

“Not sure” leaves this undecided. “Not applicable” records an exclusion, not a passed check.

Example: “I am doing this work alone; no work or information is passed to another person.”

Skills and support

Check whether people have the abilities or support needed. Examples: interviewing, analysis, writing, translation, facilitation or equipment operation.

“Not sure” leaves this undecided. “Not applicable” records an exclusion, not a passed check.

Example: “This review is limited to prerequisite dates; skill coverage is outside its scope.” Excluding this check does not establish sufficient skills.

Leave missing information unknown. If supplied figures describe different work or dates, reconcile them first. You can still create a preparation summary with no usable estimates.
