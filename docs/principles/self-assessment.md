# Principle Alignment Self-Assessment

A short reflection tool for project initiation or a health check. It supports judgment and improvement; scores do not authorize work, certify compliance, or create an approval gate.

[Open the interactive worksheet](self-assessment.html) in a browser from a downloaded copy of this repository (keep the accompanying JavaScript file in the same folder). The worksheet calculates scores and a visual profile locally, sends no responses, and stores nothing. Print or save to PDF before closing if you need a record. The tables below also support a paper or team exercise.

## Nine-minute first pass

Allow one minute to choose the project scope and read the scale, six minutes for 30 ratings, one minute to review the profile, and one minute to name an improvement. This is a design target, not a measured user-completion guarantee. Use evidence already at hand; deeper investigation belongs in follow-up. An unresolved question stays blank and prevents a complete overall score.

For solo use, record your own evidence and uncertainty. For a team workshop, have each person complete the same short first pass independently, then discuss differences and record an agreed action. Discussion and evidence gathering are additional to the first pass; do not average away material disagreement.

## Maturity scale

| Score | Evidence anchor |
|---:|---|
| 1 | No evidence of the practice in the chosen scope |
| 2 | Occasional practice; depends on individual effort |
| 3 | Defined and usually applied; gaps are identified |
| 4 | Consistently applied with traceable evidence |
| 5 | Effectiveness is checked and the practice is improved |

Use a blank for unknown, not 1 or 0. Rate observed practice, not intention.

## Score and visual summary

For each principle, average its three ratings. Show one decimal place. Average all 30 ratings for the overall alignment score (1–5); leave it incomplete if any rating is blank. Equal weighting keeps this a reflection aid, not a validated maturity model. An overall average can hide a weak principle: review the lowest complete scores and any material concern first.

In the interactive worksheet, each principle has a labeled bar and numeric score. On paper, circle the nearest marker below, keeping the exact mean in the score column. Blank means not assessed.
