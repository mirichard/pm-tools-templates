# vNext Guiding Principle Taxonomy

## Status and use

This is the candidate baseline for #741. It is machine-referenced by `schemas/principle-annotation.schema.json`, but is **not approved**. Template annotation under #742 must not begin until an independent qualified reviewer records approval. Review this taxonomy as an evolving decision framework, not a static compliance checklist.

The taxonomy synthesizes PMI's value-delivery, stewardship, stakeholder, team, tailoring, quality, complexity, risk and adaptability themes with the repository's practical, user-centered conventions. It paraphrases rather than reproduces standards text.

## Principles

### 1. Stewardship (`stewardship`)

**Definition:** Act responsibly for resources, information, people, public trust and long-term outcomes.  
**Practical relevance:** Templates should expose accountability, ethics, compliance, sustainability and record integrity rather than merely document activity.  
**Expected behaviors:** Name accountable owners; disclose assumptions and conflicts; protect sensitive information; preserve decisions and original baselines.  
**Practice mapping:** PMI stewardship principle; governance, compliance and responsible records practices.  
**Relationships:** Grounds value focus and risk optimization.  
**Tension:** Speed or convenience never justifies hiding material exposure; controls should still be proportionate.

### 2. Value focus (`value-focus`)

**Definition:** Organize decisions and delivery around beneficial, measurable outcomes rather than output volume.  
**Practical relevance:** Connect strategy, benefits, outputs, adoption and sustained outcomes throughout the lifecycle.  
**Expected behaviors:** Define value and disbenefits; name benefit owners; validate realization; stop or change work that no longer justifies investment.  
**Practice mapping:** PMI value and benefits-realization practices; product outcome management.  
**Relationships:** Requires evidence-based decisions and stakeholder engagement.  
**Tension:** Near-term financial gains may conflict with strategic, ethical or sustainable value.

### 3. Stakeholder engagement (`stakeholder-engagement`)

**Definition:** Involve affected people deliberately, inclusively and at the point their knowledge or authority changes outcomes.  
**Practical relevance:** Good artifacts make decision rights, communication needs, feedback and impacts visible.  
**Expected behaviors:** Identify affected groups; tailor engagement; close feedback loops; record dissent and consent accurately.  
**Practice mapping:** PMI stakeholder performance domain and stakeholder principle.  
**Relationships:** Supports value focus, quality and collaborative leadership.  
**Tension:** Broad participation must be balanced with timely, accountable decisions.

### 4. Systems thinking (`systems-thinking`)

**Definition:** Treat project work as part of an interconnected organizational, product and operating system.  
**Practical relevance:** Templates should expose dependencies, feedback loops, second-order effects and transitions across lifecycle boundaries.  
**Expected behaviors:** Map upstream/downstream impacts; test assumptions; track disbenefits; avoid local optimization.  
**Practice mapping:** PMI systems-thinking and complexity themes; systems engineering practices.  
**Relationships:** Strengthens risk optimization and value focus.  
**Tension:** More complete models improve insight but can delay action; model only to the decision's needs.

### 5. Adaptability (`adaptability`)

**Definition:** Tailor approach, cadence and controls to context while preserving essential outcomes and accountability.  
**Practical relevance:** Users should know when, when not, and how deeply to apply an artifact.  
**Expected behaviors:** Assess context; choose proportional rigor; revisit the approach as uncertainty changes; document consequential tailoring.  
**Practice mapping:** PMI tailoring and adaptability/resilience principles; adaptive delivery practices.  
**Relationships:** Balances stewardship and delivery flow.  
**Tension:** Tailoring must not become an undocumented waiver of quality, safety or compliance.

### 6. Quality by design (`quality-by-design`)

**Definition:** Build fitness for purpose, accessibility and verifiability into work rather than inspect them only at the end.  
**Practical relevance:** Templates need explicit acceptance, validation, usability and evidence expectations.  
**Expected behaviors:** Define done early; automate repeatable checks; prevent defects; validate with representative users.  
**Practice mapping:** PMI quality principle and project-work domain; quality-management practices.  
**Relationships:** Depends on stakeholder needs and evidence.  
**Tension:** Perfection can delay value; use risk-based acceptance tolerances without concealing defects.

### 7. Evidence-based decisions (`evidence-based-decisions`)

**Definition:** Base claims and decisions on traceable, appropriately reliable information, while making uncertainty visible.  
**Practical relevance:** Status, benefits and gate decisions require sources, calculations, assumptions and confidence—not assertion.  
**Expected behaviors:** Cite sources; distinguish fact, forecast and judgment; validate data; retain reproducible results.  
**Practice mapping:** PMI measurement performance domain; empirical and benefits-management practices.  
**Relationships:** Enables quality, learning and value focus.  
**Tension:** Waiting for perfect information can be harmful; make reversible decisions with explicit confidence.

### 8. Risk optimization (`risk-optimization`)

**Definition:** Address threats and opportunities in proportion to exposure and risk appetite to improve outcomes.  
**Practical relevance:** Controls and escalation should respond to materiality, not a one-size-fits-all calendar.  
**Expected behaviors:** Identify uncertainty early; name owners and thresholds; respond to opportunities; escalate material or ethical exposure promptly.  
**Practice mapping:** PMI uncertainty performance domain and risk principle; enterprise risk practices.  
**Relationships:** Informed by systems thinking and stewardship.  
**Tension:** Excess control suppresses adaptability; insufficient control transfers hidden risk to stakeholders.

### 9. Collaborative leadership (`collaborative-leadership`)

**Definition:** Enable people to contribute, decide and learn through clear authority, trust and shared purpose.  
**Practical relevance:** Artifacts should clarify roles without replacing conversation or collective ownership.  
**Expected behaviors:** Make decision rights visible; remove impediments; invite challenge; separate authorship from approval where practical.  
**Practice mapping:** PMI team performance domain, leadership and team principles.  
**Relationships:** Activates stakeholder engagement and continuous learning.  
**Tension:** Consensus is valuable but does not remove the need for an accountable decision owner.

### 10. Continuous learning (`continuous-learning`)

**Definition:** Use feedback, experiments, reviews and retained knowledge to improve both outcomes and the delivery system.  
**Practical relevance:** Lessons must flow into future planning, standards, estimates and template improvements.  
**Expected behaviors:** Test assumptions; record causal findings; close actions; revisit taxonomy and guidance based on evidence.  
**Practice mapping:** PMI adaptability, team and development-approach themes; retrospectives and continuous improvement.  
**Relationships:** Converts evidence into adaptation and higher quality.  
**Tension:** Frequent change can destabilize users; version shared standards and apply change control.

## Relationship map

| Decision need | Primary principles | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Approve or continue investment | Value focus + Evidence-based decisions | Stewardship |
| Tailor governance | Adaptability + Risk optimization | Quality by design |
| Resolve cross-domain impacts | Systems thinking + Stakeholder engagement | Collaborative leadership |
| Improve future work | Continuous learning + Evidence-based decisions | Preserve baseline and decision history |
