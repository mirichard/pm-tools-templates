# Product Vision and candidate assessment

**Approved by:** repo owner · **Approval date:** 09/28/2026

## Product Vision

Provide tools grounded in leading practices that help project and program managers strengthen their professional capability, achieve project and program success, and deliver meaningful outcomes.

Translate practitioner experience, established methods, and appropriate technology into free, adaptable templates, guidance, examples, and tools.

This is the enduring benchmark for all product candidates across project and program lifecycles, industries, methodologies, and planning tools. Task completion and efficiency are supporting measures; neither alone establishes professional capability, project success, or meaningful outcomes.

## Apply the vision during backlog refinement

Use a short assessment in the existing issue. Start with available evidence; add detail as a candidate approaches selection. Apply the same definitions to comparable candidates. Existing priorities, sprint assignments, and acceptance decisions require their own explicit decisions.

1. **Contribution:** Identify the user problem, which part of the vision the candidate supports, and how the proposed change is expected to contribute. Maintenance and enabling work can contribute indirectly through a named capability or obligation.
2. **Basis and evidence:** Link applicable leading-practice sources and practitioner evidence. Explain applicability and limitations. Separate observed findings, estimates, and hypotheses; a method's reputation or use of technology does not prove local benefit.
3. **Evaluation:** State what observable evidence would demonstrate the contribution. Define the measure, baseline or comparator, and acceptance criteria before evaluation. Record missing information as unknown. Agree targets rather than reuse unsupported historical percentages.
4. **Decision:** Record the Product Owner's selection, deferral, research, or rejection decision and rationale separately from the assessment. Include effort, dependencies, commitments, and other material constraints when they affect the decision.

A concise issue note can use these four labels: **Contribution; Evidence; Evaluation / next evidence needed; Decision and rationale.** Link existing material instead of duplicating it.

## Evidence and comparison rules

- **Supported:** linked evidence supports the specific finding within its stated scope.
- **Unverified:** a claim or hypothesis exists but supporting measurement or validation is absent.
- **Unknown:** the reviewed evidence does not establish an answer.

These are evidence descriptions, not priority scores. Unknown is not zero value or automatic exclusion; resolving an important uncertainty can justify research.

Compare candidates using defined measures and comparable scope, populations, and observation periods. Identify forecasts and assumptions. Do not infer that more documentation, more sources, or greater readiness means greater value. Leading-practice alignment and source availability are not proof of effectiveness.

No numerical scoring model or weights are adopted by this vision. If a model is proposed later, define its measures, scales, sources, missing-data treatment, and preference choices before applying it; examine whether reasonable changes alter the ranking. Preserve assessment results when the Product Owner selects a different candidate and document the reason.

## Planning and measurement

| Term | Meaning in this repository |
| --- | --- |
| Product Vision | The enduring purpose stated above; the benchmark for all product candidates. |
| Product Goal | A specific future outcome pursued next in support of the vision; it focuses the Product Backlog. |
| Roadmap themes and epics | Areas of investment and coordinated work supporting the vision and selected Product Goal. |
| Sprint Goal | The outcome the team intends to achieve during a particular sprint. |

This decision establishes the Product Vision. It does not select a Product Goal or change existing Sprint Goals. When agreeing a Product Goal, record the intended outcome, measure, baseline, target, and review date as appropriate. Vision alignment alone does not determine priority or establish a release commitment.

Terminology clarified by the repo owner on 09/28/2026. The existing document path is retained for link continuity.

Candidate-specific evaluation might examine whether a manager can apply a practice appropriately, make a supported decision, or achieve an agreed stakeholder outcome. These are examples, not mandatory metrics. Distinguish what the tool directly demonstrates from wider project outcomes affected by other factors.

## Research basis

The wording and lightweight assessment procedure are repository decisions, informed by these references; they are not a prescribed external scoring standard.

- [PMI purpose](https://www.pmi.org/about/purpose): project success and practitioner capability.
- [European Commission PM² methodologies](https://pm2.europa.eu/index_en): solutions and stakeholder benefits across projects, programs, and portfolios.
- [Scrum Guide: Product Goal](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html#product-goal): focus for the Product Backlog.
- [HM Treasury, multi-criteria decision analysis guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/green-book-supplementary-guidance-use-of-multi-criteria-decision-analysis/use-of-multi-criteria-decision-analysis-in-options-appraisal-of-economic-cases): transparent criteria, assumptions, and sensitivity analysis. This is appraisal guidance, not a repository compliance requirement.
