# Epic #50 — Release Manager discovery

**Research snapshot:** 2026-10-30 | **Decision status:** Recommendation for owner review; no delivery or acceptance decision

## Decision brief

**Recommend one bounded, documentation-only first delivery:** connect the maintained release-planning, release-management, dependency, communication, and closeout guidance in a short Release Manager journey guide. Do not start a 12-template suite. The canonical Release Management Template already covers planning, go/no-go evidence, rollout/recovery, communication, monitoring, and follow-up; Agile and Hybrid release plans, the cadence and continuous-delivery guides, and program-manager assets cover adjacent needs. The clearest remaining outcome is an accountable, usable path through these fragments—not eight new templates.

The v2.3 and v2.4 closeouts demonstrate repository-release decisions, evidence links, announcements, and explicit limits. They do **not** demonstrate a deployed-service rollback, service-health validation, or adoption by release managers. A small rollback-plan outline and older example thresholds warrant a separate, optional safety-focused improvement. #373 describes workflow generation and integration; #372 describes generated UAT planning and outcome capture. Neither is required to author a reusable role guide or to run the fictional validation proposed here.

**Principal uncertainty:** no observed release-manager user task, user trial, or owner-confirmed role/approval boundary establishes which cross-links or gaps are most valuable. The current public issue view also shows two open WIP PRs linked to close #50 (#1571 and #1572); neither is accepted evidence, and overlapping work must be reconciled before selection.

**Owner decision needed:** approve/revise/defer the guide candidate, confirm its intended audience and role boundaries, name the delivery owner, and resolve the open PR overlap. Keep #50 unscheduled until those gates and capacity are confirmed.

## Journey and decision handoffs

| Stage | Accountable decision / supporting roles | Inputs → outputs and handoff | Routine release | Emergency change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plan | Product owner owns outcome/scope; release manager coordinates plan/window. | Value hypothesis, bounded scope, candidate, acceptance, cadence, owners → dated or readiness-triggered plan and decision criteria. | Plan dependencies and an achievable window; a calendar date is not authorization. | Record the incident/change need, proposed scope, urgency, and named emergency authority. |
| Coordinate dependencies | Each dependency owner owns their status; release manager tracks and escalates. | Dependency, required-by condition, evidence owner, status/blocker → resolved, accepted, or explicitly held dependency. | Keep inter-team integration, support, and data/config compatibility visible. | Identify the smallest safe change and dependencies that cannot be bypassed. |
| Assemble readiness evidence | QA, security, operations, product/UAT, and other control owners attest their evidence. | Immutable candidate/source/config; test, security, acceptance, support, recovery, communication, and dependency evidence → linked evidence packet with gaps. | Verify evidence is current and for the exact candidate. | Record which risk-based checks are essential, any permitted exception authority, and mandatory follow-up; urgency alone waives nothing. |
| Go/no-go | A named release authority owns the decision; the release manager records and coordinates it. | Evidence, unresolved risks, authority, time → explicit go, hold/no-go, or conditional decision and rationale. | No release solely because checks or schedule milestones are green; unresolved mandatory evidence means hold. | Use the designated incident/change authority and record rationale and next decision point. |
| Deploy or recover | Operators execute; on-call/service owner observes; authorized release/incident authority decides promote, pause, or recover. | Approved candidate, runbook, recovery route, triggers → execution events, health evidence, and a recorded action. | Stage exposure; verify data/configuration compatibility and recovery feasibility. | Minimize exposure and preserve evidence; rollback only if safe, otherwise use an approved tested forward-recovery route. |
| Communicate | Communication owner approves audience/content; release manager coordinates updates. | Approved scope, timing, impact, status, known issues → pre/during/post messages and recipient/evidence record. | Announce verified changes and status to affected groups. | Notify incident stakeholders promptly through approved channels; distinguish known facts from investigation. |
| Monitor and collect feedback | On-call/service owner owns service health; product/benefits owner owns outcome assessment. | Baseline, guardrails, observation window/sample, support and feedback channels → health/acceptance/outcome findings and next decision. | Hold promotion when signals are missing/stale/insufficient; service health is not proof of realized value. | Monitor recovery and user impact; preserve follow-up even after service restoration. |
| Review and learn | Release manager facilitates process review; service/product owners and action owners accept their follow-up. | Timeline, decisions, incidents, feedback, health/outcome evidence → retrospective/RCA, assigned actions, next review/backlog. | Review the full cycle, not only deployment; keep outcome and operational ownership distinct. | Conduct incident/post-change review under the applicable incident process and track corrective actions. |

## Bounded delivery candidates for owner decision

Effort ranges below are planning assumptions, not commitments or historical epic estimates. They assume one documentation author and one reviewer, existing links remain available, and no new tooling, integrations, user recruitment, or production access. Actual owner and capacity are unconfirmed.

### Candidate 1 — Connect the release-manager journey to maintained assets (recommended)

**Draft title:** Story: Add a Release Manager journey guide using maintained release assets

- **User outcome:** As a release manager, I can find the right maintained plan, evidence, authority, handoff, communication, recovery, monitoring, and review record for a routine release or emergency change without searching for or duplicating a suite of templates.
- **Scope:** One concise role-based guide (candidate location: `role-based-toolkits/release-manager/README.md`) linking to canonical planning, release, quality, rollback, risk, communications, monitoring, and learning sources. Clarify role/decision boundaries, evidence custody, normal/emergency differences, and the #372/#373 boundary. Keep templates as sources of truth.
- **Exclusions:** No new workflow generator, CI/CD integration, policy defaults, telemetry, user research, live UAT, release, or claim that a real deployment was validated.
- **Observable acceptance criteria:**
  1. Each of the eight original capabilities maps to at least one maintained canonical asset, a precise gap/disposition, and an accountable role; generated, alternate, draft, and obsolete paths are not presented as additional templates.
  2. All eight journey stages specify decision owner, supporting role, minimum inputs, output/record, and next handoff; routine and emergency paths distinguish the relevant authority and evidence without implying mandatory controls can be waived.
  3. Every repository link resolves to a canonical or intentionally supported path; no generated copy is treated as the source of truth.
  4. The guide passes the two proposed fictional scenario walkthroughs below as desk checks; report them as proposed/author-reviewed checks, not user acceptance or operational tests.
- **Validation examples:** Successful fictional release reaches an authorized go decision with evidence for the same immutable candidate, then records staged health, communication, and review. Failed-readiness example holds because UAT evidence is missing or stale, or because data compatibility makes the proposed binary rollback unsafe; record the authority, communication, recovery decision, and follow-up.
- **Dependencies/readiness gates:** Owner approves audience and the Release Manager vs Product/Program Manager vs Release Train Engineer boundary; name an actual delivery owner; reconcile open WIP PRs #1571/#1572; confirm #50 does not depend on #372 execution or #373 automation. No external participant/UAT dependency for this documentation candidate.
- **Effort range:** **2–4 person-days**, assuming one short guide, link/anchor review, two scenario desk checks, and one owner review; excludes user research and any template content rewrite.

### Candidate 2 — Tighten rollback/recovery evidence in the existing records (optional)

**Draft title:** Story: Clarify release recovery evidence and non-reversible change decisions

- **User outcome:** As a release authority, I can distinguish a feasible rollback from an unsafe rollback and know when a tested restore/forward-recovery route is required before authorizing a release.
- **Scope:** Reconcile the small governance rollback outline and only the relevant legacy examples in the canonical Release Management Template. Make candidate/version, data/configuration compatibility, objective trigger, recovery owner/authority, verification evidence, communications, and the no-safe-recovery hold condition consistent.
- **Exclusions:** No rollback automation, production rehearsal, platform-specific command, new universal threshold, or change to deployment workflows.
- **Observable acceptance criteria:** The updated records require evidence for the exact candidate; distinguish binary, configuration, data, and external side effects; record an authorized recovery decision and post-recovery verification; explicitly hold when no acceptable tested recovery route exists; label example thresholds as local placeholders; preserve links and avoid duplicating the full release worksheet.
- **Validation examples:** Desk-check a reversible code-only change and an incompatible schema/external-side-effect change; the first requires tested rollback and authority, the second is held until an approved recovery strategy exists. These are proposed, not executed recovery tests.
- **Dependencies/readiness gates:** Owner confirms this is still a user gap after candidate 1; actual author/reviewer and service-policy owner must be identified before any target-specific thresholds are added. No dependency on #372 or #373 unless later scope introduces generated or executed recovery automation.
- **Effort range:** **1–3 person-days**, assuming a localized documentation reconciliation and one review; excludes environment-specific rehearsal.

**Rank and trade-off:** Candidate 1 ranks highest on user value and evidence strength because several canonical assets already cover the capabilities but have no role-level journey. Its safety benefit is high and effort low; its key uncertainty is user/owner validation. Candidate 2 targets higher operational risk but the core release worksheet already contains substantial recovery guidance, so evidence for a new standalone template is weaker; it is second and should be selected only if owner review identifies a concrete inconsistency. Creating 12+ templates ranks below both: it increases duplication and has no demonstrated usability or acceptance advantage.

## Benchmark and source limitations

Reviewed **2026-10-30**. The principles applied are: tie authorization to evidence for the exact candidate; distinguish approval from proof of readiness; make recovery safe for data/configuration and external effects; communicate verified facts; and verify service health separately from user/business outcomes. Adapt to local authority and risk—do not transplant thresholds or ceremonies.

**Authoritative guidance**

- Google SRE, [“Release Engineering”](https://sre.google/sre-book/release-engineering/), *Site Reliability Engineering* (2016). Publication year known; page-specific update date not verified in this environment. Useful for repeatable, reproducible release processes; not an organization-specific approval matrix.
- Google SRE Workbook, [“Canarying Releases”](https://sre.google/workbook/canarying-releases/), *The Site Reliability Workbook* (2018). The canonical repository template cites this guidance and records a local review date of 2026-09-20. Useful for controlled exposure and comparison to a control; does not replace business acceptance, authority, or communications.

**Public implementation/workflow examples**

1. GitHub Actions, [deployment environments and protection rules](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/workflows-and-actions/deployments-and-environments) and [manage environments](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/how-tos/deploy/configure-and-manage-deployments/manage-environments). Retrieved 2026-10-30; the page showed required reviewers, wait timers, deployment-branch rules, and secrets gated until rules pass; no page update date was shown. Demonstrates a technical approval boundary, not evidence that a human reviewed readiness or that rollback is safe. Availability depends on GitHub plan/repository visibility.
2. Azure Pipelines, [Approvals and checks](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/pipelines/process/approvals?view=azure-devops). Search surfaced the official workflow page on 2026-10-30; direct retrieval and page date were unavailable. Relevant as a stage gate separate from pipeline execution; platform-specific and not a complete recovery/communication record.
3. Argo Rollouts, [Analysis](https://argo-rollouts.readthedocs.io/en/stable/features/analysis/). Official documentation surfaced 2026-10-30; direct retrieval and page date were unavailable. Shows metric-based canary promotion/abort patterns; needs Kubernetes/metrics setup and cannot establish business approval or safe recovery of persistent data.
4. AWS CodeDeploy, [automatic rollback and redeployment](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/codedeploy/latest/userguide/deployments-rollback-and-redeploy.html). Official documentation surfaced 2026-10-30; direct retrieval and page date were unavailable. Illustrates linking deployment failure conditions to an automated action; platform configuration does not prove a specific change is safely reversible.

Direct external retrieval succeeded for GitHub documentation but was blocked for Google, Azure, Argo, and AWS pages in this session. Their URLs and source publication years (where known) are provided; current page text and update dates are explicitly unverified rather than inferred.
