This is a retrospective compatibility review, not an assessment of actual compliance, schedule feasibility or project outcomes. ## 1. U - Establish the controlled D1.1 revision before applying its commitments

**Entity and commitments:** The Coordinator and project partners rely on D1.1 for project management and deliverable procedures, and on D1.2 for their detailed implementation.

**Exact evidence:**

- SSH-11, original PDF p.1, cover: Version v1.3; actual submission 26/06/19; EC approval 18 Nov 2019. The p.11-12 headers also identify D1.1 v1.3.
- SSH-11, original PDF p.2, History: v1.3 dated 30/05/2019 is the first review; v1.4 dated 05/06/2019 is the second review; v1.5 dated 30/06/2019 is the final version.
- SSH-11, original PDF p.3, Executive Summary: the management plan is a living document intended to be updated throughout the project.
- SSH-12A, original PDF p.1, cover, and p.2, History: D1.2 v1.2, submitted 19/12/2019 and approved 03 November 2020; its v1.2 history entry concerns final review comments and submission.
- SSH-12A, original PDF p.4, Executive Summary, and p.7, section 1: D1.2 works in conjunction with D1.1, with D1.1 providing the general management overview and D1.2 everyday implementation procedures. Original PDF p.23, section 3.5.1, Table 4, references both plans as deliverable-review guidance. Original PDF p.16, section 3.1, and p.33, section 5, identify the Wiki as the place for updates.

**Applicable scope and version:** The supplied SSH-11 file is labelled v1.3 but contains history through v1.5; SSH-12A is labelled v1.2. Their printed provisions are comparable, but the exact controlled D1.1 revision and any effective updates at a particular deliverable's review date cannot be established from these records alone.

**Reasoning:** This is unresolved document identity and applicability, not evidence of mutually exclusive substantive obligations. The history may reflect unreconciled metadata or a different controlled revision; the supplied evidence does not decide which. It also does not establish that D1.2 replaced D1.1. The submission and history dates alone do not prove that an unreviewed document was submitted.

**Potential decision consequence:** A reviewer could cite the wrong revision when deciding which review requirements govern a particular deliverable. That limits a historical applicability conclusion; it does not invalidate the compatible printed workflow.

**Specific PM follow-up:** The Coordinator/document custodian should identify the exact submitted and approved D1.1 file and revision, reconcile its cover/header with its history, and link the applicable D1.1/D1.2 and dated Wiki or approved change records in the deliverable tracking record. Confirm whether any change actually affected the relevant workflow before calling it superseded. Such records may already exist; their absence from this packet is not evidence of absence from the project.

## 2. S - Preparation, review and submission stages align

**Entity and commitments:** Deliverable-producing partner, appointed reviewers, author, Coordinator and CESSDA management office.

**Exact evidence:**

- SSH-11, original PDF p.11, section 4, four production-timeline bullets: drafting/writing/completing at four weeks before the deadline; internal review starting three weeks before; final reviewed draft to the Coordinator two weeks before; PDF generated and uploaded by the Coordinator at the deadline. The draft is a Word file shared through Google Drive.
- SSH-12A, original PDF p.22, section 3.5.1, Figure 3, visually inspected: four weeks before is assigned to the responsible partner and drafting/writing/completing; three weeks before to the appointed reviewer and internal review; two weeks before to Coordinator and author and revision/final editing; deadline to the Coordinator. Figure annotations specify Word/Google Drive, mailing-list review notification and Word handoff to CESSDA MO.
- SSH-12A, original PDF p.22-23, section 3.5.1: contributors' inputs become a first draft for reviewers; after the iterative approval process, the deliverable responsible prepares the final draft for the Coordinator in Word. The management office consolidates formatting and checks English if needed; the final PDF is uploaded to the EC portal, with Word and PDF retained in the project repository.
- SSH-12A, original PDF p.23, section 3.5.1, NB: non-written deliverables require a short written description, verification means and location, using a simplified deliverable template.

**Applicable scope and version:** The generic deliverable workflow in supplied SSH-11 (labelled v1.3, identity caveat in finding 1) and SSH-12A v1.2. D1.2's non-written deliverable provision clarifies the document to prepare for that deliverable type.

**Reasoning:** Figure 3 confirms the same staged timeline as D1.1. D1.2's prose does not need to repeat every timing point to preserve it. Draft completion before review and later final consolidation refer to different stages. The management office's consolidation is compatible with the Coordinator's upload responsibility; neither source makes these roles exclusive. Word preparation and PDF submission also concern different stages. Iteration until approval does not itself authorise submission after the deadline or prove the timetable infeasible.

**Potential decision consequence:** There is no evidence-based reason to choose competing production calendars or eliminate a review stage. Treating prose and figure separately could lose the two-week handoff or misread consolidation as a changed submission deadline.

**Specific PM follow-up:** The deliverable responsible and WP leader should use the existing deliverable-process/tracking sheet to record the named owners and the four-, three- and two-week dates relative to the applicable deadline. The Coordinator should confirm the Word handoff and PDF upload responsibilities. For a non-written output, identify the accompanying description and verification/location information. This operationalises the compatible provisions rather than requiring a conflict waiver.

## 3. S - Reviewer independence, partner approval and final editing are compatible controls

**Entity and commitments:** Deliverable responsible partner, independent peer reviewer(s), involved partners, WP leader as usual approval delegate, PMB, and Coordinator/management office.

**Exact evidence:**

- SSH-11, original PDF p.12, section 4 continuation: at least one reviewer should be available; more may be chosen; the peer reviewer should come from an organisation other than those responsible for the deliverable; the approval delegate is usually the WP leader.
- SSH-12A, original PDF p.22, section 3.5.1: responsibility for production is specified in the DoA; the WP leader normally acts as deliverable responsible, with other allocations possible. Before production, that responsible partner defines contributors, quality criteria, reviewer(s) and timetable. Reviewers check consistency with plans and provide feedback and/or approval; iteration continues until all involved partners approve, followed by a final draft to the Coordinator.
- SSH-12A, original PDF pp.8-9, section 2.2.1: the PMB has responsibility for quality and final editorial review, including ensuring that WP leaders coordinate appointment of official expert reviewers with task leaders and members. Original PDF p.10, section 2.2.2, Table 2, distinguishes the beneficiary responsible for production/finalising from the selected project partner responsible for review.
- SSH-12A, original PDF p.23, section 3.5.1: final consolidation by CESSDA MO; Table 4 references both D1.1 and D1.2 as review guidance.

**Applicable scope and version:** The general deliverable-review controls in the supplied D1.1 and D1.2 versions, subject to finding 1; the actual partner/reviewer allocations for a particular deliverable are not supplied.

**Reasoning:** One independent peer reviewer is a minimum, not a statement that only one person or organisation may approve. Partner approvals and the WP leader's usual delegated approval can both be required. D1.2 does not expressly cancel reviewer independence or mandate a reviewer from the producing organisation. Its production-responsible role should not be conflated with the peer-review role. PMB quality oversight/final editorial review and Coordinator/MO consolidation are not stated to be mutually exclusive. No particular conflicting assignment or disputed sign-off is evidenced.

**Potential decision consequence:** An unsupported interpretation that D1.2 replaced independence with internal approval could weaken review; treating every reference to approval or final review as exclusive could unnecessarily block handoff. Neither interpretation is required by the evidence.

**Specific PM follow-up:** The WP leader and deliverable responsible should confirm in the existing process sheet the producing organisations, reviewer organisation(s), involved partner approvers, approval delegate, and final editorial/consolidation steps. The Coordinator/PMB should clarify those named gates if a concrete deliverable's allocation leaves their operation uncertain. Do not infer that the allocation or sign-off records are missing merely because they were not supplied.

## 4. S - Delay notification and explanation requirements are cumulative

**Entity and commitments:** Deliverable partners, WP leader, Coordinator, Project Officer and PMB.

**Exact evidence:**

- SSH-11, original PDF p.12, section 4 continuation: delays should be communicated in advance with the WP leader and Coordinator, and plausible explanations offered to the EC with upload of the final version.
- SSH-12A, original PDF pp.23-24, section 3.5.2: the WP leader must email the Coordinator a detailed explanation at least three weeks before the deadline; the Coordinator consults the Project Officer and informs the PMB and responsible partners of the result; the next official periodic report must include the explanation. The explanation format identifies the deliverable and due date, requests an extension to a proposed new date, and describes reasons and consequences.
- SSH-12A, original PDF p.32, section 4.2: partners report risk situations immediately to their WP leader, including deliverable timing; the Coordinator is consulted for problems/delays, with further escalation if unresolved.

**Applicable scope and version:** Possible or actual delayed deliverables under the supplied management and quality plans. The three-week advance notification is a delay-management requirement, distinct from the three-week start of routine internal review in Figure 3.

**Reasoning:** A specified advance-notification threshold is compatible with the broader requirement to communicate in advance. An explanation can be supplied during consultation, with final upload and again in periodic reporting. None of these provisions says that one communication replaces another. A requested extension is not proof of an agreed revised deadline. The immediate-risk provision also prevents inferring that a risk discovered later should be withheld simply because the three-week point has passed. The records do not establish any actual late-discovered delay or approved exception.

**Potential decision consequence:** Treating periodic reporting as a substitute for advance consultation or upload-time explanation could omit a documented step. Treating an extension request as approval could cause use of an unconfirmed deadline. Neither outcome is established as having occurred.

**Specific PM follow-up:** The WP leader should use the existing tracking process to record the advance notice and detailed explanation. The Coordinator should record the Project Officer consultation/result, retain the applicable deadline, and ensure the explanation accompanies the relevant upload and next periodic report. If a risk first appears inside the three-week window, notify/escalate immediately and ask the Coordinator to document the handling and consultation outcome; do not presume an automatic extension.
