# DEV-SSH-01: condition C, development run 3

No supported conflict is established within the deliverable preparation, review and submission boundary. The supplied procedures can be satisfied together. One version-control clarification is necessary before treating this comparison as a determination of the rules applicable to a particular deliverable at a particular time.

Classification: C = supported conflict; S = consistent; V = valid revision or different scope; U = unresolved. These classifications concern documented commitments, not actual compliance or project health.

## 1. Authoritative D1.1 revision and applicability require confirmation — U

**Entity and commitments.** The Coordinator and project partners need an identifiable management baseline for deliverable production and review. D1.1 describes itself as a living management document; D1.2 describes complementary operational procedures and references both plans for deliverable quality.

**Exact evidence and locations.** SSH-11, original PDF p.1, cover, labels the document v1.3, records actual submission on 26/06/19, and EC approval on 18 November 2019. Its p.2 History lists v1.3 on 30/05/2019 as “First review”, v1.4 on 05/06/2019 as “Second review”, and v1.5 on 30/06/2019 as “Final version”; the running header remains v1.3. SSH-11 p.3, Executive Summary, calls the plan a living document to be updated continuously. SSH-12A pp.1–2 identify v1.2, submitted 19/12/2019 and approved 3 November 2020. SSH-12A p.4, Executive Summary, says it operates in conjunction with D1.1; p.7, Introduction / Purpose and Scope, distinguishes D1.1's general management overview from D1.2's everyday implementation guidance. SSH-12A p.23, §3.5.1, Table 4, references both plans for compliance with templates and guidelines. SSH-12A p.16, §3.1, and p.33, Conclusion, place updates in the SSHOC Guidelines Wiki.

**Scope and version.** This concerns identification of the supplied D1.1 artifact and the applicability of both plans to a selected deliverable and review period. It does not establish that every recorded revision or every approval date represents a simultaneously operative baseline.

**Reasoning.** The D1.1 cover/header and history leave the precise identity of the approved text unclear. D1.2's later approval does not, by itself, establish replacement of D1.1, especially given its express cross-references. Nor does a final-version history date after the cover's submission date establish that a deliverable was submitted without approval. The available evidence does not resolve document labeling, effective dates, or later procedural updates. This is an unresolved baseline question, not proof of incompatible procedural obligations.

**Potential decision consequence.** A PM cannot use the supplied cover dates alone to determine which procedural revision governed a particular handoff or to decide that D1.1 requirements were superseded.

**Specific follow-up.** The Coordinator/document custodian should identify the canonical approved D1.1 file and explain the v1.3/v1.5 discrepancy, then provide the effective procedural baseline and any relevant authorized change or Wiki history for the deliverable under review. Record whether the referenced production and review provisions changed. These records may exist; their omission from the packet is not evidence that they do not.

## 2. Production, review and submission timing align — S

**Entity and commitments.** The responsible partner prepares the deliverable; appointed reviewers conduct internal review; the Coordinator and author carry out final editing; the Coordinator submits.

**Exact evidence and locations.** SSH-11 p.11, §4, specifies completion of drafting/writing four weeks before the deadline, commencement of internal review three weeks before, delivery of the final reviewed draft to the Coordinator two weeks before, and Coordinator generation/upload of the PDF at the deadline. SSH-12A p.22, §3.5.1, Figure 3, visually shows the same four checkpoints: “4 weeks before”, “3 weeks before”, “2 week before”, and “Deadline”. Its responsibility row assigns these stages respectively to the responsible partner as listed in the GA, appointed reviewer, Coordinator and author, and Coordinator. The activities are drafting/writing/completing, internal review, and revision/final editing; annotations specify Word sharing through Google Drive, reviewer notification by mailing list, and sending the Word version to CESSDA MO. SSH-12A pp.22–23, §3.5.1, requires a final open Word document to CO, CESSDA MO consolidation, PDF portal upload, and storage of both Word and PDF versions.

**Scope and version.** These are general deliverable procedures in the supplied SSH-11 artifact and SSH-12A v1.2, subject to finding 1's applicability qualification. Figure 3 is a workflow, while SSH-11 p.11 Figure 1 is a project milestones/deliverables calendar; the latter is not an alternative review-stage sequence.

**Reasoning.** The same stage timings and handoffs appear in both plans. “Final and reviewed draft” before Coordinator editing does not mean that final formatting or editorial work has already ended. D1.2's iterative review until involved partners approve does not inherently contradict the scheduled handoff: both can be satisfied if the review completes before that handoff. The records do not show an actual deliverable whose review could not finish in time. The named office's consolidation work can support the Coordinator's submission responsibility.

**Potential decision consequence.** There is no evidence-based reason to choose between competing four-/three-/two-week schedules or to change the submission owner on the basis of these documents.

**Specific follow-up.** The deliverable responsible partner and WP leader should check the relevant deliverable process sheet against this common sequence, identify the named reviewer and Coordinator handoff, and have CO confirm the submission arrangement. This is operational confirmation, not a request to cure a demonstrated schedule conflict.

## 3. Reviewer independence, partner approval and final editing are compatible controls — S

**Entity and commitments.** The responsible beneficiary/WP leader, independent peer reviewer(s), involved partners, PMB and Coordinator have different preparation, review, approval and editorial responsibilities.

**Exact evidence and locations.** SSH-11 p.12, continuation of §4, requires at least one available reviewer, allows more, specifies a reviewer from an organization other than those responsible for the deliverable, and says the approval delegate is usually the WP leader. SSH-12A p.22, §3.5.1, says the DoA identifies the responsible partner, the WP leader normally acts as deliverable responsible, and roles may be defined differently. Its procedure requires advance definition of reviewers and quality criteria, circulation of the first draft, reviewer feedback and/or approval, iteration until all involved partners approve, and then preparation of the final draft for CO. SSH-12A pp.8–9, §2.2.1, gives PMB responsibility for final editorial review and for ensuring WP leaders assign expert reviewers. SSH-12A p.10, §2.2.2, Table 2, assigns production/finalising to the DoA beneficiary and review to a selected project partner. SSH-12A p.23, §3.5.1, assigns final formatting and any needed English check to CESSDA MO.

**Scope and version.** General quality and deliverable-specific workflow provisions in the supplied plans. No appointment or approval record for a particular deliverable is supplied.

**Reasoning.** A selected project partner can be from a different organization than the producer. D1.2 does not authorize self-review or abolish D1.1's independence requirement. A WP leader can coordinate production or act as approval delegate while independent reviewers assess the work and involved partners give their approval. “Usually” and “normally” do not create exclusive assignments. PMB editorial accountability and Coordinator/office editing can coexist; neither passage states that only its named role may act. The text does not establish that WP-leader approval alone replaces involved-partner approval.

**Potential decision consequence.** A PM should not infer that later wording permits same-organization peer review, a reduced approval gate, or removal of PMB editorial accountability. The evidence also does not support claiming that any actual appointment breached these controls.

**Specific follow-up.** The WP leader and deliverable responsible should document the named reviewer(s), their organizations, the involved partners whose approval is needed, and the approval delegate in the existing process sheet. CO and PMB should confirm how their editorial review and final formatting steps are recorded for that deliverable. Confirm any documented exception through the applicable governance record rather than assuming one.

## 4. Delay notification and explanations are cumulative, not competing alternatives — S

**Entity and commitments.** Partners/WP leaders notify the Coordinator about delays; CO communicates with the Project Officer and informs the relevant project parties; explanations accompany subsequent reporting.

**Exact evidence and locations.** SSH-11 p.12, continuation of §4, requires advance communication with the WP leader and Coordinator and plausible explanations to the EC with upload of the final version. SSH-12A pp.23–24, §3.5.2, requires the WP leader to email CO a detailed explanation at least three weeks before the deadline; CO consults the PO and informs PMB and the responsible partners of the result; the explanation is included in the next official Periodic report. The p.24 template asks for the deliverable and due date, requested extension date, reason and consequences. SSH-12A p.32, §4.2, additionally requires immediate partner reporting of risk situations to the WP leader and Coordinator consultation for problems or delays.

**Scope and version.** Delay handling for project deliverables under the supplied procedures, not a determination of any actual delay or extension approval.

**Reasoning.** A specified three-week notice refines the general requirement for advance notice. Explanation at upload and explanation in the next Periodic report are separate occasions that can both be satisfied. Consultation and a request for extension are not evidence of a granted extension. A problem first discovered inside the three-week window would need case-specific handling, but no such event is established here; the immediate-reporting provision means silence until another checkpoint is not justified.

**Potential decision consequence.** Treating periodic-report disclosure as a substitute for earlier notification or for the upload explanation would discard a compatible commitment. The documents do not justify treating the original deadline as changed merely because a request was submitted.

**Specific follow-up.** For a deliverable with a possible delay, the WP leader should give CO the prescribed explanation and proposed extension; CO should record the PO consultation result and arrange the relevant upload and Periodic-report explanations. If the issue was discovered within three weeks of the deadline, record when it became known and seek CO's immediate handling decision without claiming timely notice occurred.
